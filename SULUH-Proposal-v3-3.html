Why 35B, not 70B or 100B

A 70B dense model at FP16 requires ~140 GB of weights plus 100+ GB of KV cache — around 250 GB total — which exceeds a single 256 GB Mac Studio when OS overhead and concurrent models are included. A 35B MoE achieves comparable quality with 3B active parameters per token, fitting comfortably in 60–100 GB and leaving headroom for QLoRA jobs, evaluation, and burst load. Full-scale 100B pre-training is positioned as a Phase-2 vision contingent on national compute partnership (NAIO / MyDIGITAL), not a Year-1 deliverable.

4Three-track model strategy

SULUH runs three parallel tracks matched to what each tier of infrastructure can verifiably do — from anonymised cloud teachers down to campus laptops.

Track A — Cloud teacher (API, no PII)

Models: Kimi K3 (Moonshot), Claude Opus 8 (Anthropic). Role: generate curriculum-grounded synthetic training data from anonymised rubrics and model answers, and arbitrate quality on golden-set evaluation. Data flow: anonymised curriculum → cloud API → synthetic corpus → downloaded to cluster for local adaptation. Cost: ~RM 3,000–5,000 over 12 months. Critical rule: no raw student PII or assessment data ever leaves UPSI.

Track B — Sovereign teacher (on-premise, 35B MoE)

Model: Qwen3.6-35B-A3B (Apache 2.0, 262K–1M context). Hardware: Mac Studio 256 GB via EXO layer parallelism. Pipeline: Stage 1 synthetic corpus (M1–2) → Stage 2 continual pre-training on Malay/English (M2–4) → Stage 3 instruction tuning on grading & advising (M4–6) → Stage 4 federated adaptation on faculty-local documents (M6–9) → Stage 5 DPO alignment (M9–10) → Stage 6 accreditation-readiness evaluation (M10–12).

Track C — Federated student (campus laptops, 0.8–2B)

Models: Qwen3.5-0.8B and Qwen3.5-2B. Role: edge-deployed tutoring, local adaptation, federated research. Training: distilled from the 35B teacher via SFT; federated LoRA across 10,000 campus laptops under APA-EF differential privacy. Only adapter deltas are transmitted — never raw student data.

5Algorithmic contributions

The swarm is not just infrastructure — it is the project's primary research engine. Because laptops train models they can genuinely hold (0.8–2B), each contribution can be evaluated end-to-end on real heterogeneous hardware at a scale — 10,000 devices — few labs can replicate.

ContributionWhat it doesWhere it runs

Foundational proof: Psyche

Psyche (Nous Research) has demonstrated that distributed training of transformer models is feasible across untrusted internet participants, using a coordinator/client consensus protocol with blockchain and TCP backend coordination. Psyche proves the core premise SULUH's federated track relies on: transformer models can be trained across many independent nodes without high-speed interconnects or a single coherent memory space.

SULUH operates under a strictly easier trust assumption — all 10,000 devices are institutionally owned — which enables APA-EF differential privacy (impossible in untrusted networks, where no node can be trusted with sparsification parameters) and LES-GA curriculum alignment (which requires a shared educational knowledge base only institutional ownership provides). SULUH's contribution is not proving distributed training works. Psyche proved that. SULUH's contribution is showing how to deploy it safely, privately, and educationally within a sovereign institutional boundary.
